# HackMIT GiveCampus challenge context

## Submitted challenge prompt

> You have been provided a dataset about school constituents. Create something interesting that helps your school's fundraising team!

A few potentially interesting examples follow, but participants are welcome to build something different.

### Constituent segmentation

Find groups of people who have a few things in common—for example, people who were in school at the same time, participated in the same club, attended an event, live within 30 miles of one another, or are young recurring donors. Pitch a potential next action for that group, such as inviting them to a local event or asking them to support a campaign connected to a group in which they participated.

### Next action

Find the next best action for each constituent. Build a system that recommends the most useful next step for each person: send a thank-you message for a gift, send an event invitation, send a reunion mailer, ask for a gift, or take another appropriate action.

### Hidden dollars

The university needs to raise an additional $1 million this year. Identify where opportunity might be hiding in the database. Possibilities include donors who gave previously but have not been asked in the past few years, or constituents who received major promotions but are still being asked for the same amount as 10 years ago.

### “Who do I contact?” copilot

Give the user a natural-language interface for finding people in the dataset. A fundraiser might ask, “Show me all alumni in Boston who attended an event in the past year.” A more ambitious version might answer, “Who are the top 20 people I should contact before the big Giving Day, and why those 20?”

## Agreed interpretation for review

The challenge is not limited to static data-analysis submissions. Data visualizations, interactive dashboards, web applications, copilots, recommendation tools, and other useful experiences are encouraged. Reviewers should assess whether the data supports those product-oriented directions as well as conventional analysis.

The original challenge framing has already been submitted. This engineering review should prioritize improving the dataset and participant guidance rather than rewriting the core prompt.
